02 · ELECTRIC OWNERSHIP

Make EV ownership feel familiar.

Build a charging routine around your home, your regular journeys and the exact vehicle. The details below are a planning guide; charger compatibility, tariffs and rules should be checked for your location.

1 · Start with parking and permission

Check who controls your parking space, ask your apartment association or property owner about installation rules, and consult a qualified electrician or charger provider before adding a dedicated circuit. Confirm earthing, cable routing, meter arrangements and safe access.

2 · Understand AC and DC charging

Home and destination charging commonly use AC equipment; public rapid charging uses DC. The car’s onboard charger, battery limits and connector determine actual charging speed, so a higher-rated post does not guarantee a higher rate for every vehicle.

3 · Match the connector to the car

Before driving to a station, check connector type, rated power, operator access, payment method and whether the location is open to the public. Use the owner’s manual and charger listing to confirm compatibility.

4 · Plan with a comfortable reserve

Use your normal route, not just the brochure range. Speed, traffic, gradients, temperature, payload, tyre pressure, cabin heating or cooling and battery condition can change energy use. Check a second charging option on longer trips.

5 · Compare total running costs

Estimate electricity cost from energy used and the tariff that actually applies to your home or charger. Include public charging fees, installation, parking, service and insurance when comparing with a petrol or diesel car.

6 · Care for the battery as the manual says

Follow the vehicle maker’s charging and storage guidance. Avoid assuming one charge limit fits every battery or use case; pay attention to heat, long storage periods, software updates and warranty conditions.

7 · Know what happens on a long trip

Check whether a station has the required connector, whether it is operational and how billing works. Leave time for queues or a slower charge, and do not rely on the map’s listing as live confirmation.

8 · Keep the end-of-life path in view

Ask the manufacturer or authorised service network about battery warranty, diagnostics, repair and take-back. India’s Battery Waste Management Rules place obligations on producers and formal collection channels; do not discard EV batteries with household waste.

Official reference: Government of India, Ministry of Power, EV charging-infrastructure guidelines (2024) ↗. For battery handling, see the Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022 ↗. General planning information only; confirm current site-specific and vehicle-specific instructions.

03 · RESPONSIBLE OWNERSHIP

Good ownership reaches beyond the drive.

Safety, maintenance and lower-impact choices reinforce each other. Start with the vehicle manual, local rules and qualified service advice for your car.

Maintain to the maker’s schedule

Follow time- and distance-based service intervals, use the specified fluids and parts, and keep invoices. Address warning lights, leaks, tyre damage and braking issues promptly rather than relying on a dashboard reset.

Keep tyres safe and efficient

Measure pressure when tyres are cold and follow the door placard or owner’s manual. Check tread, cuts, uneven wear and alignment; under-inflation can affect handling, rolling resistance and tyre life.

Drive with fewer abrupt changes

Look ahead, build speed progressively, leave room to coast and brake smoothly. Combine errands where practical and avoid carrying unnecessary weight or idling for long periods.

Choose repairs with care

Use competent technicians and parts appropriate to the car. Ask for a clear estimate, retain service records and repair safety-related faults before cosmetic work.

Use energy and consumables thoughtfully

Switch off accessories when not needed, keep the air-conditioning system maintained and dispose of oils, filters, tyres and batteries through appropriate collection channels.

Return batteries through formal channels

Do not place vehicle batteries in household waste. Contact the manufacturer, service centre or authorised recycler about collection, refurbishment and recycling; battery chemistry and safe handling procedures vary.

Consider the full life of the vehicle

When choosing a car, compare size, real use, energy source, repairability and expected ownership time. A smaller or more efficient vehicle can reduce use-phase energy, but manufacturing and electricity sources also matter.

Retire vehicles responsibly

For end-of-life vehicles, check the current rules and authorised facilities in your state. Use recognised scrapping channels and keep the deregistration or disposal paperwork.

Official references: India Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022 ↗ · CPCB end-of-life vehicle resources ↗. This guide is educational and does not replace the owner’s manual, a mechanic’s advice or local requirements.
